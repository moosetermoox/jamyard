# PART 1 — DeKoven's philosophy, in quotable form

## Coliberation (his coined word)

> "**Coliberation — A shared transcendence of personal limitations, of our understanding of our own capabilities; a sudden, momentary transformation of our awareness of the connections between ourselves, each other, and the world we find each other in.**"

> "It's a word I coined so I could describe to people what a **'good meeting'** is like."

> "Coliberation is what happens when we work or play extraordinarily well together… we actually experience ourselves sharing in something bigger than any one who is present. This is what I call the experience of the **'Big WE.'** It's a corollary to the 'Big ME' experience of self-transcendence. **If the Big ME is the 'peak experience,' Coliberation or the Big WE, is like becoming a whole mountain range.**"

> "**You just can't liberate yourself by yourself. You can't be free if you're the only one. You can meditate, but you can't separate. You can become one only if you become 'one with.'**"

> "**Coliberation is not the opposite of codependence. Coliberation is why we become that way.** … Just as we now understand how we can sicken a relationship by becoming too dependent on each other… we now need to rediscover how we can heal a relationship by **setting each other free**."

He names two **boundary conditions** that are a direct spec for failure modes in a class activity:
- **Alienation** — "you're all playing in the same orchestra… but you don't really feel a part of it. It's like nobody is really listening to you, nobody is even aware of you."
- **Conformity** — "You are so much part of the group… that you find yourself doing things that you, personally, would never do."

— https://www.deepfun.com/coliberation/

## The well-played game

> "**A well-played game is one that is appreciated by all the players, regardless of score or distance to the goal. One that in fact must be appreciated by all players. By definition.**"
> "**The quality of 'well-played' doesn't describe the game itself, but rather how that game was played**, enacted, performed. **Even a game with a truly lousy design can be well-played.**"
— https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/having-fun/201602/the-lousy-game

The ping-pong origin story is the whole thesis in anecdote form — and it is *literally a handicapping story*:

> "Bill knew that I couldn't really play ping pong. And I knew that he could really, really play. … Bill suggested that **I just try to hold my paddle still enough so that he could get the ball to hit it.** Apparently, that was more than challenge enough for the both of us… **If it hadn't been for Bill's expertise, and compassion, and for our mutual willingness to find a way to play that allowed both of us to play together, at our best,** I would never have reached the understanding of what being part of a well-played game was all about."
— https://www.deepfun.com/coliberation/

## The play community vs. the sports community — **the single most useful passage on the site**

> "**In the sports community, the rules and officials decide if the players are good enough to play. If not, they change players.**
> **In the play community, the players decide if the game is good enough to play, if not, they change rules. For them, the rules are always negotiable, the ultimate criteria for success being not so much who won, but much more, how much fun they were able to create for each other. Every game in the play community is continually being designed.**"
— https://www.deepfun.com/more-thoughts-on-coliberation-part-two-changing-the-game/

Junkyard Sports is the same claim about sports:
> "**In conventional sports, the game determines whether you're good enough to play. In junkyard sports, the players determine whether the game is good enough.**"
> "**There's a big difference between a sport that you learn and a sport that you make up.** Sports that you learn… **have a way of separating people. There are those who are good enough, and those who aren't.** … But when you're making up a new sport, the question isn't so much about whether you are good enough to play it. **It's about whether the sport is good enough to make you *want* to play.**"
> "**There is no need to adapt a sport for a specific population when the very population that will be playing it is creating it.**"
— https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/introducing-junkyard-sports · https://junkyardsports.com/media/JunkyardSports.pdf

## Safety, trust, and quitting

> "**We need, in order to be willing to be willing, some guarantee, somewhere, that no matter what happens in our pursuit of the Well-Played game, we will not be risking more than we are prepared to risk.** Even though I'm aware that I might die as a result of trying to climb this mountain with you, I can accept that as part of the game. **On the other hand, when I discover that you're cutting my rope so that you can get to the top first, I find myself much less willing to play.**"

> "The motto of the New Games Foundation was 'Play Hard, Play Fair, Nobody Hurt.' **The 'nobody hurt' part was probably the most important of the three, because if people couldn't feel safe enough to play, they generally couldn't play.**"

His chain is: **willingness → safety → trust → familiarity → conventions.**

> "**Play is something you do because you want to. You can't be forced to play… It's something that you do because you are free, and free not to.** … **Nothing stills the collective fears and soothes the conceptual nerves as making sure people know they have the freedom to quit at any time.**"
— https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/having-fun/201512/helping-people-feel-safe-enough-play

> "During our weeklong Deep Fun workshops, we always make it the rule that **people can quit, whenever they want to, without apology or explanation**… Usually by the second or third hour, **we conduct an hour of 'Quitting Practice.' People are encouraged to try quitting even when they don't feel especially like quitting, just so they can feel what it's like, and so we can establish the practice and permission.**"
— https://www.deepfun.com/on-quitting/

Peter Gray, quoted approvingly by DeKoven, gives the mechanism: "**Freedom to quit is the most basic freedom in all true play.** … **Since the game requires a certain number of players, everyone who wants the game to continue is motivated to keep the other players happy so they don't leave.**" — **Exit rights are what make the other players' happiness your problem.** https://www.deepfun.com/hunter-gatherers-pick-up-games-and-the-play-community/

## Inclusion — the non-negotiable

> "**My one overriding goal was to create a theater in which everyone is an actor.** I could see no purpose, none at all, for excluding anyone from a game, **even if the exclusion was only until another unfortunate goose fails at her appointed round.** So this became a rule for me, and has remained so my entire career. **Everybody who wants to play gets to play.**"

And on what exclusion *feels* like: "one might compare the mush pot experience to **a momentary taste of aging, illness, incarceration, staying after school, being in school**: there are all these games and people playing them in orbit around your personal space. **And you don't get to or have to play any of them.**"
— https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/having-fun/201507/life-in-the-mushpot

## Changing the game mid-play

> "**It's easier to change the game than to change the players.**"
> "They evolve **because you let them evolve**, because you… **encourage the players to try different ways to play them, just for the fun of it — especially when the way they're playing doesn't seem as fun as it was a few minutes ago.**"
> "**This art — adapting to the audience and the moment — is what makes the evolving game evolve, and is key to everything I teach about teaching games.**"
— https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/having-fun/201604/the-evolving-game

**The well-timed cheat** — a real design primitive:
> "There are many strategies for designing a game as it is being played. **One is most often called 'cheating.'** … the most effective kind of cheating is when the cheater actually **makes the game more fun, for all the players**. In the book, I call it '**the well-timed cheat**,' because it is usually put into practice **at the moment it is most needed — just when the game isn't as much fun as it should be, when people are getting too serious about winning or losing, when they are getting hurt, physically, emotionally.**"

**Quitting as a group gesture:** "sometimes, all you need is one person to quit. **Because by quitting, that one person reminds everyone that quitting is possible, that the game itself is something that is being played only because everyone wants to play it.**"

## Goal of all games

> "**And the victory that you attain across the set of all games isn't winning all the games: it's being invited to play.**"
— DeKoven quoting Jordan Peterson and adopting it as his own thesis, https://www.deepfun.com/games-cooperative-contd/ and https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/having-fun/201701/even-competition-is-cooperative

## The New Games canon (for a design doc)

- **"Play Hard. Play Fair. Nobody Hurt."** (attribution contested — Brand in the 1976 book per most sources; DeKoven reorders it to "Play Fair, Play Hard, Nobody Hurt")
- Brand's entire ruleset for Earthball, 1966: "**There are two kinds of people in the world: those who want to push the Earth over the row of flags at that end of the field, and those who want to push it over the fence at the other end. Go to it.**" — and then the players spontaneously invented the anti-goal: "**members of the pushing team defected, rushing around to the other side of the ball and pushing it back the way they had just driven it.**" (Turner, *Games and Culture* 1(1) 2006, quoting NGB p.9)
- Brand: "**You cannot change a game by winning it, you cannot change a game by losing it, you cannot even change it by refereeing it.**"
- Farrington: "**People could center on the joy of playing, cooperating, and trusting, rather than striving to win.**" (NGB p.10)
- The six tenets, incl. "**No one should be left out, eliminated, or unable to play**" and "**Games are living culture, adapted and changed as required.**"
- Sports Illustrated 1976 on the first tournament: "**There were no experts, no hard and fast rules, no memories of past humiliations… no ribbons, just banners and balloons; no spectators, just players.**"

**And the movement's own post-mortem — the most important warning for a platform whose whole pitch is teacher-authoring:**
> "**The one aspect of New Games that we had the most difficulty communicating was the idea that people could create their own. The New Games movement largely became based on a fixed, and eventually closed repertoire of games. That was very much contrary to what we wanted to communicate.** The most luck we had was when, during the New Games Trainings, we asked people to **try to combine two sports and see what they came up with.**"
— DeKoven, in Berlinger, https://jergames.blogspot.com/2008/02/history-of-new-games-foundation.html

# PART 2 — The reusable design operators

This is the highest-density part of the research: the New Games / Junkyard corpus, read structurally, yields a small set of **rule transformations** that turn any competitive structure into a no-winner one. Most are config-level toggles.

| Operator | Where it comes from | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Conversion, not elimination** | Prui, Blob, Vampire, Octopus, Monarch, Snake in the Grass, Hagoo | The "out" set *is* the growing team. Population conserved; game self-terminates. |
| **Power trade on conversion** | Monarch (rooted, but gets the ball), Octopus (frozen, but becomes a tentacle) | Being caught changes *what* you may do, not *whether* you play. |
| **Handicap the strong role, not the weak** | Snake in the Grass (snakes crawl, runners run) | Asymmetry favors the many; nobody is individually singled out. |
| **Universal constraint** | Siamese Soccer; DeKoven's "try it with the wrong hand or hold hands with somebody" | Flattens skill *without naming anyone as weaker* — the dignity property. |
| **Loser gets the director's chair** | People to People, Broken Spoke, Go-Tag | The failure state is the most agentic role in the game. |
| **Cheap, repeated loss** | Stand-Off, Hunker Hawser (~10s rounds) | High loss frequency destroys the meaning of any single loss. |
| **Self-refereeing constraint** | Dho-Dho-Dho (one breath), Human Spring (your own reach) | No adjudication needed; the clock is legible to everyone. |
| **Defection as meta-rule** | Earthball; NG-tournament Tug of War with side-switching | Whoever is winning loses members. Equilibrium is the point. |
| **Optional scoring overlay** | Human Spring: "for fun, *or* compete for furthest distance" | `scoring: none \| score` — never baked in. |
| **Fun written into the loss condition** | Slaughter: "**If you or your opponent stops enjoying the action, you have both lost the game.**" | The well-played game as a formal rule. |
| **Sanctioned micro-violation** | Prui's "**minimal cheating**" — permission to blink your eyes open camera-shutter-quick | An anxiety valve that keeps people from opting out entirely. |
| **Constructive quitting** | DeKoven's six named sideline roles | Leave the play surface without leaving the game. |
| **De-personalize the sides** | *Froggie Boogy* → Happy Frog / Sad Frog | Keep the race, the dice, the winner — but **nobody owns a side**, and input is pooled. The game has a winner; the players don't. |

**"Constructive quitting" in full** — this is a literal spec for what a student who taps Pass should be offered:
> "You can stand near the sidelines and be **a coach**. You can be a **scorekeeper**. You can be a **timekeeper**. You can **help other people who want to play find their way into the game**. You can be the **sportscaster**. You can **cheer**. You can be a **cheerleader**."

**DeKoven's "7 Ways to Make Almost Anything More Fun"** — the source post is dead, but he quotes the first three in the coliberation essay and restates the fuller set as the Junkyard Sports "Play by Your Own Rules" sidebar:
1. If there are two sides, **add a third or take one away**.
2. **Change sides** every now and then — when someone's ahead by two, when someone throws a 9, when somebody has to go to the bathroom.
3. If there are turns, **take them together**, at the same time, as in "1, 2, 3… go" — or skip a turn.
4. If there's a score, **keep playing until you discover who the second winner is, or the third, or the last. Or give each other points. Or play without points.**
5. **Add** another ball / goal / rule — or **take a rule away**, or **borrow a rule from another game**.
6. **Challenge yourself:** wrong hand, or hold hands with somebody else.

"**Give each other points**" is a three-word rule change that inverts a competitive game into a generosity game and needs no new machinery.

**DeKoven's de-elimination worked example** (Who Stole the Cookie → "Numbers" → "Rollover") gives three named moves: **lower the memory load** (names → numbers); **remove the skill gate that isn't the point** (drop the rhythm; keep only "you can't take too long" — deliberately *judged*, not measured); and **replace elimination with demotion — and make the demotion ripple** ("moving down to the end… makes everyone below your original number have to change their number as well, which adds significantly to conceptual delight and chaos").

# PART 3 — Structures, mechanics, and concrete adaptations

## 3.1 SYNCHRONY / GROUP ATTENTION

### Zen Counting / Count to 20 — *already shipped as your `one-voice` phase*
Circle, eyes closed, count to 20; anyone may say the next number; two people at once = restart from 1; nobody says two consecutive numbers.
**Mechanic:** shared-fate attention with **zero content** — nothing to be good at, nothing to know, so subject-matter status and confidence stop mattering. The only skill is modelling what everyone else is about to do. And the failure is **nobody's fault in particular**.
**Two upgrades you don't have:** (a) In person, eyes-closed is hard mode; on phones everyone is *already* eyes-closed to each other, so your tap version is inherently at the hard end. If 30 kids can't clear it, the correct softening is to **add a channel back** — a coarse, laggy "N thumbs hovering" pressure meter on the host screen — not to lower the target. (b) A `sequence` param is free content: count backwards from 20, spell the alphabet, count by 3s, name the months, recite the periodic table.
https://dramaresource.com/count-to-20/ · https://www.teampedia.net/wiki/Zen_Counting · https://leadershipheights.org/trails/zen-counting/

### Mind Meld — **build this**
Pairs. "One… two… THREE" and both say any word simultaneously. Two unrelated words. Count again; both simultaneously say a word that *bridges* the previous two. Repeat until both say the **same word at the same moment** → celebrate, start fresh. No repeating any word already said. Explicitly bad with three people — keep it pairs.
**Mechanic:** **convergence under simultaneity.** Because both commit at the same instant, neither can converge by *conceding* — only by modelling the other's mind. Success is therefore evidence of genuine mutual understanding, which is why the celebration is disproportionate. Failures are funny and generative, so there's no losing move, only a longer path. Typically lands in 3-6 rounds.
**Adaptation — and it's strictly better in your medium.** In person the simultaneity is enforced socially and cheating is trivial (a fractional delay lets you hear your partner). On a server it's *guaranteed*. Concretely: `collect` with `assign:"pairwise"` + `simultaneousReveal` in a loop, plus one new thing — a **match test** (case-fold, trim, strip articles/plurals; borrow Just One's identical-clue spec) to decide loop exit. The no-repeats rule becomes a server check against the pair's history, which is *easier* than in person. **The host screen is the whole show**: a projected wall of 15 pair-cards lighting up "MELDED ✨ (Eiffel Tower) — round 4," with a class artifact at the end ("11 of 15 pairs melded, average 4.2 rounds"). That shared-fate layer doesn't exist in the physical game, where each pair is private.
https://wiki.improvresourcecenter.com/index.php?title=Mind_Meld · https://improvgames.com/convergence/ · prior art: https://mindmeld.fyi/

### Synchro Clap → "tap when you think your partner will"
Two people make eye contact and clap *simultaneously*; the receiver eye-contacts someone else, repeat, accelerate.
**Mechanic:** the only pair-level *simultaneous act* in the improv canon — the closest thing to the lab manipulation in the synchrony literature.
**Adaptation:** true rhythm over school wifi is a lost cause (50-400ms jitter exceeds the perceptual window). But this is latency-*tolerant* because the server doesn't need true simultaneity, only *intent* simultaneity. **It is `adjudicateTap` with the sign flipped**: reward collision instead of penalizing it, widen the window to ~600ms, and score "how close were you?" as a class average. Cheap, and it reuses your one-voice machinery wholesale.
https://improvencyclopedia.org/games/Synchro_Clap.html

### The evidence base (for PEDAGOGY.md — cite honestly)
- **Rennung & Göritz (2016)**, meta-analysis of **60 experiments**: interpersonal synchrony → prosociality, **medium effect**. https://opus.bibliothek.uni-augsburg.de/opus4/files/97119/2151-2604_a000252.pdf
- **Wiltermuth & Heath (2009)**, *Psych Science* 20(1): synchrony → more cooperation even at personal cost; mechanism is attachment, and **positive affect is explicitly not necessary**. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19152536/
- **Valdesolo & DeSteno (2011)**, *Emotion* 11(2): synchronous partners perceived as **more similar to the self**, evoked more compassion and more costly helping. The best belonging citation. https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21500895/
- **Kirschner & Tomasello (2010)**: the children's study. https://www.eva.mpg.de/documents/Elsevier/Kirschner_Joint_EvolHumBeh_2010_1552719.pdf
- **Counterweight — cite these too:** a children's field experiment finding **no effect** (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8707737/), and an active expectancy-effects critique (https://direct.mit.edu/opmi/article-pdf/doi/10.1162/opmi_a_00067/2064593/opmi_a_00067.pdf).
- **Crucially: no study in this literature tested network-mediated tapping.** The mechanism in Valdesolo & DeSteno is *perceptual*. **Claim the structure — shared fate, no winners, mutual attention — not the neuroscience.**

## 3.2 ONE SHARED OUTPUT

### Exquisite Corpse / Consequences — **you are one feature away**
Successive collaborators each add to a single artifact seeing **only the tail of the previous contribution** (paper folded after each turn). Surrealist origin 1925; the name is the first sentence produced, *"Le cadavre exquis boira le vin nouveau."* Grammar-template variant: "The [adj] [noun] [adv] [verb] the [adj] [noun]." Drawing variant divides a figure into head/torso/legs/feet.
**Mechanic:** **enforced partial information produces an artifact nobody authored and nobody could have predicted.** That does two jobs at once — it removes authorial responsibility (participation floor on the ground) and makes the reveal a genuine surprise *to the makers*. **The fold is the game.** Remove concealment and it becomes ordinary collaboration: less funny, more status-inflected.
**Adaptation:** `collect.rotateFrom` is already the fold-and-pass, and `finish-my-drawing` is picture-consequences. What's missing is **`revealTail`** — show only the last N words, or mask all strokes above a y-cutoff on the pad while keeping them in the data so the final assembly is whole. **This is the single highest-value small feature in the whole research doc**, because it converts an existing primitive into a distinct, famous, no-winner game. The grammatical-template variant needs *nothing new* — a 6-step rotation chain where each student fills one slot blind, 30 poems assembling in parallel on the projector via `reveal-one`. Zero AI calls.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exquisite_corpse

### Renga / tanrenga — the high-status version of the same primitive
Japanese linked verse: alternating **5-7-5** and **7-7** stanzas by multiple poets. The compositional rule is that **each stanza links only to the one immediately before it and must NOT link back further** — so there is no overall narrative, only a chain of local connections, and no single poet can impose a plan.
**Mechanic:** same information constraint as Exquisite Corpse, arrived at aesthetically. The two alternating forms give **two clearly different jobs** (open a move / close a move), which is a far better scaffold for a reluctant writer than "write a poem."
**Adaptation:** `rotateFrom` chain + a *non-blocking* client-side syllable counter (show "5 / 7 / 5" filling as they type, never reject). Show only the previous stanza and the link-forward-only rule enforces itself. Five parallel chains of six students → five 12-stanza renga in 12 minutes, side by side on the projector. **`tanrenga` (two stanzas) is trivially shippable today**: pairwise collect, A writes 5-7-5, B answers 7-7. Real ELA curricular cover, which matters for adoption in a way "count to 20" doesn't.
https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renga

### Word-at-a-Time Story → **"Conducted Story"**
Circle; a single story, one word each. Coaching notes: much harder than it sounds; seed with a familiar story for novices; expect saboteurs smuggling in atomic bombs. *Story Story Die* adds an MC who cuts mid-word and eliminates hesitators.
**Mechanic:** **radical constraint on contribution size.** One word is small enough that nobody freezes and small enough that nobody can hijack.
**Adaptation:** your `relay` phase is already this, but two source-flagged fixes matter. (a) **Turn-by-turn with 30 students is a pacing disaster** — the source's own remedy is "divide into two circles," so run **5-6 parallel chains of 6**, all growing on the host screen. (b) **Story Story Die's conducting mechanic is the pacing fix, and it survives without the elimination**: a 5-8s per-turn timer with auto-pass. **The cut is the clock, not a judge** — nobody is eliminated and nobody is judged, but you keep the seamless-continuation thrill. Add a `seedStory` param ("we're all telling Cinderella"); the coaching notes say it measurably improves novice output.
https://improvencyclopedia.org/games/Word_at_a_Time_Story.html · https://improvencyclopedia.org/games/Story_Story_Die.html

### Mandala — DeKoven's own one-shared-output game, and it drawing-adapts cleanly
"Ask people to make up their own mandala-like shape (divide into appropriate size groups as necessary)… **Ask the mandala-people to hum or make chant-like sounds once they've achieved their group mandalatude.** Take pictures. If people want to play again, encourage them to create a whole new mandala-like pattern." Then: "after people are sufficiently overwhelmed and amused by **their collective incompetence**…"
**Mechanic:** a shared visual artifact with **no correct answer and no scoring**, plus an explicit signal for "we're done" that the group produces collectively. "Amused by their collective incompetence" is the entire emotional target.
**Adaptation:** groups of 4-6; each member draws one stroke-set onto a **shared** canvas in rotation (or simultaneously, into assigned wedges of a radial layout), building one symmetrical figure. The host screen shows 6 mandalas assembling live. No score, no vote — the phase ends when each group taps "we're done," and the reveal is the gallery. This is `merge` semantics applied to the drawing pad rather than a text draft.
https://www.deepfun.com/mandala-game/

### Liberating Structures worth stealing (all CC BY-NC, Lipmanowicz & McCandless)

- **1-2-4-All** (12 min) — silent write 1' → pairs 2' → foursomes 4' → each group shares **one** idea 5'. **Already shipped as Snowball.** Two refinements you don't have: the **"one idea, no repeats"** cap on the All step (they deliberately limit to 3-4 shared ideas and forbid duplication — that's what keeps the share from dragging), and the explicit instruction to **write** during silent reflection, not just think. https://www.liberatingstructures.com/1-1-2-4-all/
- **25/10 Crowd Sourcing** (~20 min, **min group size 15** — exactly your class size) — everyone writes their boldest idea **and its first step** on a card; cards are **passed around unseen**; five rounds of "read the card in your hand, rate 1-5, write the score on the back, **do not look at previous ratings until you've decided your own**"; sum to max 25; countdown reveal "Who has a 25? A 24?"; close with "**Are you holding a wild card that scored low but is intriguing?**" **Mechanic: anonymous distributed rating with *blind* scoring** — the don't-peek rule is the entire anti-conformity mechanism, and the countdown is suspense with no loser (a low card is a "wild card," not a failure). **LS explicitly says do NOT do this online — and your architecture makes the hard parts trivial**: random redistribution is a shuffle, blind scoring is enforced by *not sending* prior ratings to the client, five rounds is your `rate` phase in a loop, and the countdown is `reveal-one`. **Strong recipe candidate: you get the one structure they wrote off as un-digitisable.** https://www.liberatingstructures.com/12-2510-crowd-sourcing/
- **TRIZ / Creative Destruction** — "agree the **worst possible result**; alone, list everything you could do to *achieve* it; combine in groups; **reckoning**: identify everything you're currently doing that resembles the list; decide what to stop." **Mechanic: permission through absurdity.** "How could we make this class terrible?" gets honest answers that "how could we improve this class?" never gets, because the first is a joke — and the reckoning quietly converts the joke into a real audit. **The safest way I know to get students to critique a classroom.** Pure text, three collects with a merge, zero AI, and LS itself blesses the chat-based fallback. https://www.liberatingstructures.com/6-making-space-with-triz/
- **Troika Consulting** — trios, rotating client role. Client presents 1-2'; consultants ask clarifying questions only, **no advice yet**; **the client turns away** and listens silently while the consultants discuss 4-5'; client turns back and shares what was valuable. **The turn-away is the entire structure** — removing the client from the conversational loop kills the defensive reflex and frees the consultants to be candid. **In text the turn-away is free and perfectly enforced**: the client's device shows "Your consultants are talking. Sit tight," while the two consultants get a shared draft (`merge`). Trios from `team-split groupSize:3`, three rounds as a loop. Shippable. https://www.liberatingstructures.com/8-troika-consulting/
- **Min Specs** — generate max specs → combine → **cross off anything that doesn't achieve the purpose**; test question: *"If you followed all the min specs except this one, could you still achieve your purpose?"* Tip: **"praise the groups that drop the most."** **Mechanic: subtractive convergence** — the group's output is defined by what it *deletes*, which produces much less turf-defending than brainstorming. **Adaptation: your `checklist` phase inverted** (check = delete instead of check = done), so `engine/phases/checklist-state.js` already has the membership/attribution/live-progress machinery. Great for "what are the actual rules of this classroom," "what does a good essay actually need," lab safety, group charters. https://www.liberatingstructures.com/14-min-specs/
- **Fist-to-Five** — fist = block, 1 = suggest changes, 2 = minor issues, 3 = pass without discussion, 4 = good idea, 5 = **I'll volunteer to lead it.** **Mechanic: one gesture carries both agreement level *and* commitment to act**, cast simultaneously so nobody anchors on the first responder. **Adaptation: a 6-button `collect-choice` with simultaneous reveal and a host distribution bar** — the fastest possible whole-class temperature check, and a natural companion phase after any discussion. **Two cautions:** do *not* implement a real block for teenagers (one student holds the room hostage), and keep the 0-5 legend permanently on the projector. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_decision-making

## 3.3 SHARED FATE — the group succeeds or fails together

### The Mind (Wolfgang Warsch, 2018) — **strongest single recommendation in this report**
Deck of 100 cards. Level N deals each player N cards, held secret. **The only rule: all cards must be played face-up onto one pile in ascending order.** Any player may play at any time; no turn order. **No communication whatsoever** — no talking, no counting, no hints. If a card is played while anyone holds a lower one, the team loses a life and all lower cards are discarded. Lives are finite; losing them all loses the game. **Throwing star:** any player may propose one; if the vote is **unanimous and silent**, everyone simultaneously discards their lowest card.
**Mechanic: the only communication channel is time itself.** A player holding 7 waits a heartbeat; a player holding 94 waits a long time. Everyone must calibrate to a **shared subjective tempo** — and the calibration is mutual, because I have to guess how long *you* think a long time is. Failure is structurally blameless in exactly the way Zen Counting's collision is. **It is Zen Counting with information asymmetry bolted on.**
**Adaptation — better than the physical game:**
- The medium *is* the mechanic. It's already a game about latency; a server with millisecond timestamps adjudicates it perfectly where a table of humans argues about who played first.
- **The physical game's biggest weakness — enforcing "no communication" — is solved for free.** In person, a raised eyebrow is cheating and nobody can police it. On separate devices with a projected host screen, **the silence is architectural.**
- **It scales to 30 where the cardboard caps at 4**, and that's an opportunity: (a) whole class, 1-2 cards each — deal 30 students one number from 1-100 and the class must play all 30 in order, a 3-minute round of near-unbearable tension; or (b) parallel tables of 4 with a class-level life pool.
- **Implementation:** a new phase (`ascend`) that is the same *shape* as `one-voice` — server-authoritative, tap-driven, pure adjudication function with injected clock, success/failure, celebration, auto-advance. Hold `{hands, pile, lives, stars}`; on `play-card`, pop the player's lowest, compare against `min(all remaining hands)`, advance or dock a life and purge. ~60 lines, unit-testable like `adjudicateTap`; `simulate-one-voice.js` is the sim template. The throwing star ports onto `merge`'s `agreeMode` unanimity gating and is worth keeping — it's the game's only *cooperative decision* and its most bonding moment.
- **Host screen: no player identities during play.** Reveal who was holding what only after a life is lost.
- **Curriculum reskin (this is where it earns class time):** the cards don't have to be numbers. Deal each student a card object with a display label and a hidden sort key — events in chronological order, fractions by value, vocabulary alphabetically, historical figures by birth year, atomic numbers, book chapters. **The ascending-order rule is content-agnostic**, so a party game becomes a review activity with no loss of tension.
https://the-mind-game.com/rules · https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mind_(card_game)

### Just One (Spiel des Jahres 2019) — **build this second**
Active player takes a card without looking and picks a number 1-5 → that's the Mystery Word, visible to everyone but them. Everyone else secretly writes **one single word** as a clue. Reveal simultaneously; **all invalid clues AND all identical clues are cancelled** (same word family, singular/plural, or the same word misspelled). If everything cancels, the card is lost. The active player gets **exactly one guess** or may pass. Correct +1; pass -1; **wrong -2** (this card and the next). Team score out of 13; try to beat your own record.
**Mechanic — one of the most elegant social mechanics ever designed.** Duplicate-cancellation converts "give a good clue" into "give a good clue **that nobody else will think of**," which forces every player to simultaneously model the guesser *and* the rest of the group. It punishes the obvious answer, so the safest play is the wrong play — **it forces originality without ever asking for it.** And when three people independently write the same clue, that cancellation is *itself* delightful: the failure is a moment of connection ("we all thought PARIS!"), not a loss. **Nobody is ever singled out as bad at the game.**
**Adaptation — it is already a text game.** Every physical component is text on a small surface; "close your eyes" is a screen that doesn't render; there is nothing spatial, physical or rhythmic to lose. You have the hard parts: `collect` + `simultaneousReveal` is the secret-write-then-reveal, `preview` is the teacher gate, active-player rotation is a `foreach` over players. **The one new piece is a pure `engine/phases/clue-cancel.js`** — case-fold, trim, strip accents, collapse singular/plural and word-family, Levenshtein ≤1-2 on stems for "Rythem." The rulebook gives you the exact spec.
**Scale to 30 two ways:** parallel groups of 5-6 with a class total, **or** whole class with one guesser, leaning into mass cancellation as the joke — "31 clues written, 24 cancelled, 7 survive," with the duplicate piles stacking visibly. **Option two is genuinely novel (impossible with cardboard)** and produces a great projector moment: a bar chart of the six things this class collectively thought of.
**Curriculum reskin is immediate:** the mystery word is vocabulary, a science term, a character from the novel. Clue-writing is **retrieval practice with a creativity constraint**, and cancellation surfaces which associations are *shared class knowledge* vs idiosyncratic — genuinely diagnostic for the teacher. Best "review game that doesn't feel like a review game" in this document. Keep the -2/-1 asymmetry; it's what makes passing an interesting *group* decision.
https://x.boardgamearena.net/data/rules/justone/JO_EN_Rules.pdf

### Wavelength → cooperative class swarm
Hidden target on a spectrum ("Hot ↔ Cold," "Underrated ↔ Overrated"); the Psychic gives **one clue that lies on the spectrum**; the team argues and turns a dial; points by proximity.
**Mechanic:** it externalises the fact that **words are fuzzy and gradient**, and makes the *argument about the gradient* the fun.
**Adaptation — drop the two teams.** One student (or the teacher) is the Psychic; the **whole class individually places a slider**; the host screen reveals **all 30 markers as a swarm** plus the true target. Score the class average's distance, or "how many landed inside the band." Your `estimate` phase already has the numeric-guess-and-distribution machinery. **Where it gets pedagogically valuable:** the spectra don't have to be silly. "Definitely a metaphor ↔ definitely a simile." "Purely physical change ↔ purely chemical." "The narrator is reliable ↔ unreliable." "This action was justified ↔ unjustified." **The swarm reveals class disagreement as a *shape*** — a far better discussion starter than a majority vote. It's the continuous version of your `sort` phase, and the continuum is more honest for genuinely gradient questions.
https://www.ultraboardgames.com/wavelength/game-rules.php

### Extract-the-mechanic, skip the game
- **Hanabi** — turn-based, 2-5 players, 30-40 min, steep curve. Don't build it. **Steal the clue economy:** "clues are a scarce shared resource, and spending one is a group decision." Drop a clue budget into any guessing activity you already have — the class has 5 hints for the round, anyone can propose spending one, the host screen shows the remaining budget. One counter and a propose/confirm interaction converts a guessing game into cooperative resource management.
- **Codenames Duet** — 2-player. **Steal the asymmetric key:** two halves of the class see different information about the same projected item set and must reconcile. ("Half of you can see which of these are mammals; half can see which are native to Europe; together, find the three that are both.") Sits on top of `sort`/`match`/`rank`.
- **So Clover!** — the resolution phase is spatial and awkward at scale, **but the clue-writing half alone is a shippable 8-minute vocabulary activity today**: give each student 4 word-pairs, they write one connecting word for each. Drop the clover geometry and present resolution as "here are 5 word cards and 4 clues — match each clue to its pair," which is your `match` phase with a decoy.

## 3.4 NOTICING AND APPRECIATING OTHER PEOPLE

### The twelve mechanics that make appreciation feel real rather than forced
Distilled across six literatures (compliment circles, Warm Fuzzies, Positive Discipline, Cool Kid, restorative circles, Tootling, RBSE, Appreciative Inquiry, Aron):

1. **Story, not adjective — enforced by required fields.** The most consistent finding anywhere. **Tootling requires three fields: helper / helpee / description of the observed behavior.** RBSE forbids the bare trait. Nelsen forces "I would like to compliment ___ **for** ___" and diagnoses the alternative: *"When students say 'I like the way ___', it is more about them than the other person."* **A one-word compliment box produces worthless output; a mandatory "what happened" field is the cheapest possible quality gate.**
2. **Prior noticing time.** Cool Kid's day of watching; Tootling's card on the desk all day; Bessick's between-session reminders. **Appreciation improvised on the spot is generic; appreciation retrieved from memory is specific.** Run it as the harvest of a prior collection window, not a cold event.
3. **A far→near escalation ladder, so nobody chooses how exposed to be.** LS Positive Gossip does it in 9 minutes: round 1 = gossip positively about **an absent mentor** (zero risk, teaches the register); round 2, new partner = **someone in the group**; round 3, new partner = **your partner**. By round 3 the words are already in their mouth. **The single most directly stealable structure in this cluster.**
4. **Receiver silence + averted attention.** Warm Shower's back-to-the-group; Hot Seat's *"listens only — without justifying or discussing."* Kills deflection, reciprocity pressure, and negotiation. **It converts appreciation from a transaction into a testimony.**
5. **Third-person / overheard grammar** (Positive Gossip, Behind Your Back). Easier to give, lands harder — it borrows the credibility of overheard gossip.
6. **Convergence beats eloquence.** RBSE needs ~30 stories from ~10 people before *patterns* — the actual mechanism — exist. One flattering story is dismissible; ten independent ones aren't, and **the receiver does the synthesis.**
7. **Source diversity.** The adolescent RBSE field experiment found feedback from professional-only sources **decreased** positive emotion. **Appreciation from someone with no obligation to give it is worth more.** Peers > teacher; unexpected peers > friends.
8. **A visible remaining-pool display.** Compliment Circle's legs-out/legs-crossed marker turns "who *deserves* this?" (a social-preference question) into "**who's left?**" (set membership). Cheapest anti-popularity device found anywhere — and it's literally a host-screen roster that greys out.
9. **Pass as a first-class, non-penalized move — and passing still counts as being acknowledged.** Restorative circles: *"every person in the group is acknowledged, whether they speak or not."* Plus Nelsen's third legal move — **"ask for a compliment"** — with a **2-3 second choice timer**, added explicitly *"otherwise it starts looking like a popularity contest."*
10. **Playfulness as an authenticity guard.** LS: *"Encourage exaggeration and lightheartedness, mimicking the patterns of negative gossip."* Solemnity is what makes mandated appreciation feel fake.
11. **Non-contingency.** Harvard EASEL's Cool Kid states it as a hard rule: *"**Has to be random and all children must be Cool Kid the same number of times. Should not be contingent on behavior, grades, or anything else.**"*
12. **Persistence beyond the moment.** Take-home note, certificate with the three most meaningful, yarn bracelet.

### Four concrete adaptations

**(a) "Warm Shower" as your highest-coverage appreciation phase — build this.**
Groups of 4; each round one member is the target. **The target's device shows nothing while the other three type** — that's the averted gaze and the back-turned chair, for free and perfectly enforced. Host screen shows only "Round 2 of 4 — 45 seconds." Then the target's device reveals all three at once. **Coverage math: 30 students = 8 groups × 4 rounds ≈ 16 minutes, 90 appreciations delivered, zero students missed.** Best appreciations-per-minute-per-student structure found anywhere, it's silent enough for a real classroom, and it maps onto `team-split groupSize:4` + a foreach over group members + `collect` with a per-target visibility mask.

**(b) Tootling as your appreciation *input format*, everywhere.**
Three required fields — **who helped / who they helped / what happened**. "Sam is nice" is not a valid tootle; "Sam noticed Ana couldn't find her ruler and gave her his" is. **Adjectives are cheap; incidents are not.** There's no fixed slate of nominees, no scarcity, and — the key property — **the positive-only framing means the bottom of the distribution has no expressible form.** This is the only strand in the whole appreciation literature with controlled evidence that structured peer appreciation actually **raises the sociometric status of the specific excluded kids** (Murphy & Zlomke 2014, 24 studies / 48 cases). **Do NOT inherit the reward contingency** — the behaviourist protocol reinforces the *reporting*, which manufactures insincere tootles, and the public cumulative chart is a leaderboard by another name.
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40617-014-0025-0

**(c) Positive Gossip's three-round ladder as a 10-minute recipe.**
Round 1 (pairs, 3 min): positive gossip about **a mentor or supporter in your life**. Round 2 (**new** pairs): something positive about **someone in this room**. Round 3 (**new** pairs): something positive about **your partner**. Close: "How did it feel to go through the rounds?" LS's own noted online weakness is getting a fresh mix each round — **which is exactly what `engine/phases/pairing.js` and `rotatePairsFrom` already solve.** You are ahead of the source. CC BY-SA 4.0, legally reusable with attribution. https://www.liberatingstructures.com/positive-gossip/

**(d) Kindness Buddy → a guaranteed-coverage derangement.**
Random secret assignment, week-long window, chained reveal ("My kindness buddy is ___, and my act of kindness was ___").
**Mechanic: a derangement guarantees every student both gives and receives exactly once — coverage is mathematical, not socially negotiated.** Secrecy removes the reciprocity trade. **Two failure modes the sources don't solve and software can:** (i) the **silent-buddy problem** — if your assigned giver forgets, you receive nothing and find out *publicly*; (ii) random derangements form several disjoint cycles, so the yarn dead-ends. **Fix both by forcing a single Hamiltonian cycle at assignment (Sattolo's algorithm, O(n))** and by giving the teacher a private mid-activity "these 3 students have zero" panel with a backfill route.

### Hard constraints — three findings that should become validator rules
- **Never ship "who is MOST X."** Even flattering superlatives produce a public ranking with a visible tail of zero-vote students, and **every student can count their own votes.** The harm vector isn't the wording, it's **the public zero count.** Tootling is the safe inverse: same noticing, no scarcity, no expressible bottom.
- **The sociometrics evidence is two-sided, and the binding constraint is the second half.** Measured-harm studies (Bell-Dolan 1989; Mayeux 2007) found essentially **no** harm from like-most/like-least nomination. But Zakriski et al. (1999) found **only 40% of schools consented**, objections were "at times strong," two teachers returned the form blank, and families declining said they "did not want their child stigmatized." **You are not running an IRB study with debriefing — you're projecting results on a wall.** https://mitch.web.unc.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/4922/2013/10/Zakriski-et-al.-1999.pdf
- **Roster-position bias is real and asymmetric.** Poulin & Dishion (2008): students higher on an alphabetized roster received *more* nominations, and the effect was **~2× stronger for "like least" (r = −.31) than "like most" (r = −.14).** **Shuffle any roster, per device, always.** https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC2812902/
- **Teacher preview before the projector** was independently added by every practitioner working above grade 3.
- **Aron's control condition is the design lesson for the whole platform.** Same pairing, same 45 minutes, same alternating turn structure, small-talk content ("What are the advantages and disadvantages of artificial Christmas trees?") → *"any effect of this procedure is **not** simply a matter of putting two people together in any kind of structured interaction for 45 min."* **The control condition is exactly what most icebreaker apps ship. Structure alone does nothing; the content is the intervention.** Effect size of the real version: **d = .88.** https://psychodramaaustralia.edu.au/sites/default/files/falling_in_love-aron.pdf
- Middle-school replication: Echols & Ivanich (2021), N=301, 6th-8th grade, students paired with someone they didn't know and didn't consider a friend — **equally effective for boys and girls, and equally for cross-ethnic as same-ethnic pairs.** Deployable kid-adapted question bank: https://ggie.berkeley.edu/practice/36-questions-to-help-kids-make-friends/
- **One interaction worth designing around (Aron Study 3):** extraverts got much closer than introverts when closeness wasn't named as the goal, but *"**when getting close is made an explicit task, introverts became as close as extraverts.**"* **Do not be coy about what the activity is for.**

## 3.5 ABSORPTION GAMES — physical originals whose social logic transfers

These are the New Games core. None of them survive literally (they're all milling-around-a-room games), but **the absorption engine is a phase-graph pattern**, and it's the pattern DeKoven cared about most.

### Prui — the crown jewel
Everyone mills with eyes closed. On bumping, shake hands and say **"Prui."** If you get "Prui" back, that person isn't it — move on. **One person is secretly the Prui: when you shake their hand they say nothing and don't let go.** You're now Prui too — open your eyes, stay silent, offer your free hand. Ends when everyone is Prui.
> "It gets quieter and quieter while **the plaintive sounds of the unpruied few** saying 'Prui' becomes more and more distinct as they attempt to merge with **the invisibly giggling many**." — DeKoven

**Mechanic: absorption, not elimination — the "out" state and the "in" state are the same state. Losing your search *promotes* you into the group.** The tension curve is inverted from a normal game: it gets quieter and lonelier for the *shrinking minority*, which is exactly what produces the release when the last person joins. And **the group's success is legible without any scoring** — you feel it in your hand.
**Adaptation — "Silent Prui," and it works:** every student gets a secret two-word phrase. Each round they're paired with someone and both submit their phrase; identical phrases = **merge into one growing cluster and adopt the cluster's phrase**. One student's phrase is seeded as the Prui. The host screen shows **only the count** — "24 still searching, 6 joined" — never names. The joy is the reveal at the end: **who was holding onto whom.** Uses `rotatePairsFrom` + `simultaneousReveal`, plus a growing-set state. And **implement "minimal cheating" as a real feature**: a Peek button that reveals one bit (e.g., "is anyone near you already Prui?") — DeKoven's own anxiety valve, sanctioned rather than policed.
https://www.deepfun.com/prui/ · https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/having-fun/201511/the-game-prui

### Blob / Vampire / Octopus / Monarch / Snake in the Grass
Tag where the tagged **join the tagger** rather than leaving. Monarch adds the power trade: hit by the ball → you're rooted in place, **but you get the ball**. Octopus: frozen fish become **tentacles**. Snake in the Grass: converts *and* handicaps the powerful role (snakes must crawl).
**Mechanic:** *"You become what you fear."* **The set of losers is the winning team.** And the difficulty ramps automatically as more convert, so the game ends on its own without a timer.
**Adaptation:** this is a **general elimination-phase replacement**, not one game. Anywhere your engine currently eliminates, offer `onEliminate: "convert"` — the player joins the other side with a **different action set** (they now judge, or write decoys, or hold the clue budget) rather than watching. That is the single highest-leverage change to the existing `eliminate` phase, and it's directly DeKoven's rule.

### Lap Game
Tight circle, everyone facing the same way, everyone sits simultaneously onto the knees behind them. **It either stands or it doesn't — there is no partial success.**
**Mechanic: pure symmetric mutual dependency.** The structure collapses if any single person defects, and nobody is holding anyone up more than anyone else. **This is the physical archetype your `one-voice` phase already is the text version of** — worth saying explicitly in your docs, because it's the lineage.
> "each person freeing the other to transcend her fears, each person being freed. **And even if you failed, it was fun. Funny fun.**"

### People to People
Everyone pairs; a caller shouts accumulating body-part connections ("right hand to left knee!"), pairs hold them, absurdity accumulates, then "People to People!" and everyone scrambles for a **new** partner. **The one person left without a partner becomes the next caller.**
**Mechanic: the "out" player is promoted to director.** Being unpaired isn't a penalty — it's the only route to the most powerful role. **The cleanest agency-shift primitive in the whole corpus**, and DeKoven notes the delicious tension of people *pretending* to search hard because they secretly want to be caller.
**Adaptation — this is a phase-type idea, not a game.** Any pairwise phase with an odd class: instead of `oddHandling:"sit-out"` or `"triple"`, add **`oddHandling:"caller"`** — the unpaired student gets the *authoring* seat for that round (they write the prompt every pair must answer, or choose which of three prompts the class gets). Rotate it. **Your odd-student-out problem becomes your most desirable role.**
DeKoven on why it works: *"it's up to them how crazy they want to get or not. **And because it's up to them, you really don't have to worry about offending anyone or being too personal.** If they don't feel comfortable enough, they won't do it."* — https://www.deepfun.com/person-to-person/

### Go-Tag
Line of squatting players; the chaser may **tap any squatting player and shout "Go!"** — that player instantly becomes the chaser. "The key is to change chasers often and rapidly."
**Mechanic: distributed relay of the active role.** Nobody has to be fast for long; a slow player can be a devastating chaser for three seconds. **Everyone in the line is *live* — you might be tapped at any instant — so there are no spectators even though most people are sitting still.**
**Adaptation:** a `relay`/`turn` variant where the current writer can **hand off mid-turn** to anyone, and the recipient must continue instantly. Solves the "29 kids watch one text box" problem by making everyone tappable. Pairs beautifully with the Conducted Story timer.

### Earthball / Slaughter — the two strongest no-winner precedents
Earthball: two stated goals, and **players defect to whichever side is losing**, so the ball goes back and forth all afternoon. Slaughter: *"**If you or your opponent stops enjoying the action, you have both lost the game.**"*
**Mechanic: the win condition is subordinated to the fun condition.** In Earthball the meta-rule (keep the game alive) beats the stated rule (score); in Slaughter, enjoyment is written into the *loss* condition.
**Adaptation:** an `autoDefect` toggle on any two-sided phase — **whenever one side leads by N, the next student to submit is assigned to the trailing side.** Free, one line, and it's the purest expression of the whole tradition.

# PART 4 — The facilitator / "playmaker"

**Terminology note:** "playmaker" is **not** DeKoven's word — a site search returns nothing. His vocabulary is **play community / fun community / play leader / referee / playmate**. The New Games Training Manual uses *playmate*: "**Every other person becomes a potential playmate.**"

## The two-referee choreography — a direct spec for your architecture

> "**New Games was far more than a collection of games. The focus of our trainings was not just to develop repertoire, but also to develop a uniquely coliberating style of leadership.** The New Games Referees were trained to work in pairs. In general, one Referee would act as the '**high visibility Referee**,' getting people to come play, teaching the rules, kindling enthusiasm; while another Referee would be '**low visibility**,' **explaining the game to stragglers, inviting people in or out of the game, playing the game and thereby modeling how to play.**"
>
> "We rarely if ever had only one game going — **always at least two games, always balancing each other.** If one game was high energy, the second game was low. If one game was semi-competitive, the other semi-cooperative. **The idea was always to provide a choice, always allow people to quit one game and find another.** As soon as enough people were playing, **the high-visibility referee would leave** it to start another somewhere else."
— https://www.deepfun.com/more-thoughts-on-coliberation-part-three-new-games/

**The translation is almost too neat:** your **host screen is the high-visibility referee** (rules, enthusiasm, energy, the projected show). Your **teacher console is the low-visibility referee** (catching stragglers, letting people in and out, quiet moderation). **And the high-visibility referee's success condition is *leaving* once enough people are playing** — which is a real argument for host screens that get out of the way mid-phase rather than narrating continuously.

## Choice as the mechanism (New Games Training Manual, quoted by DeKoven)

> "**Essential to the Tournament model is the idea of choice. Choice in play is a vital element in all of New Games. Choice means some control over experience, giving a greater sense of both freedom and safety.** … More importantly, they can choose to follow the games as suggested or, **in response to the open facilitation style of the referee, to change the games to personally affect what happens.**"
>
> "**As a result, a democratic system of play is created. Each player is given support for choosing what he or she wants, for taking risks, for transcendence, for becoming an individual; at the same time, through the same process, his or her membership in the group is strengthened.**"
— https://www.deepfun.com/choice-proposal-play-community/

**That last paragraph is the thesis of the whole facilitation tradition: individuation and belonging are produced by the same mechanism — choice.**

## Boal's Joker — the "difficultator"

> "**The joker's function is not that of facilitator, the joker is (in Boal-speak) a difficultator, undermining easy judgments, reinforcing our grasp of the complexity of a situation, not letting that complexity get in the way of action or frighten us into submission or inactivity. Things aren't always what they seem, it says; let's try and do something about them.**" — Adrian Jackson, translator's intro to *Games for Actors and Non-Actors*

> "Boal prefers to say the Joker is a 'difficultator' — **making things more complex, rather than easier** ('facilitator' is related to *facile*). … **Boal insists that the joker decides nothing. The joker lays out the rules of the game, but the audience are free to change them.**"

The Joker's formal Forum Theatre duty is to **spot an implausible "magic solution" and hand the ruling to the audience** — identify the problem, refuse to adjudicate it. And the older Arena Theatre Joker System is explicitly Brechtian: it **"makes the act of authorial creation visible."** **A good host screen shows the seams rather than performing seamless magic.**
https://www.laurelfelt.org/difficultator/ · https://ceasefiremagazine.co.uk/augusto-boal-games-techniques/ · https://www.critical-stages.org/1/playing-seriously-with-augusto-boal/

## Handling the student who won't play — DeKoven's site, best writing on this anywhere

Michael Bean on **counter-games**:
> "They might develop a counter game that is antithetical to it… often it simply appears to be an attempt to play a game outside the game and players… **They're simply playing — just not with us.**"
> "**No matter the apparent aim of the counter game, I find it helpful to always assume there is some kind of fear going on inside those who are failing to play with.** This assumption might not always be right, but **making it keeps me from being mad at them or mentally labeling them 'trouble-makers.' Once I've labeled them I can't understand them better… I can't possibly like or understand a label — only a person.**"
> Remedies: "**I can drop back to a less threatening game. I can put it to the group what we should do to help the counter players feel more like including themselves… I can stop the game and address the fear** — '**You're right, I'm sorry. I pulled us all together into a group and started explaining this game as though you had all been playing it together for years. I apologize. Let's slow down a bit and ease in to this better.**'"
— https://www.deepfun.com/counter-games/

## Improv side-coaching (Spolin, via Gary Schwartz)

> "**The sidecoach is at once a fellow player, a grounded teacher and a canny director.**"

## Liberating Structures' stance on facilitator power

- "A good invitation is **precisely ambiguous** — clear enough that everyone knows what's being asked, open enough that many different answers are possible."
- "**As host, don't over-help or overcontrol the group's conversations.**" · Must-not-do: "**Over-help or over-control the work of others.**" · "**Specify minimum constraints and let go of over-control. Use the power of invitation.**"
- But also the opposite failure: Must-not-do: "**Unleash people without structure** such as a clear purpose or minimum specifications."
- **Every microstructure spec has "Participation Distribution" as a mandatory named field** — "everyone has an equal opportunity to contribute," and "there is no upper limit on the number of participants." That's worth copying literally into your recipe schema.
- "**Novice hosts can follow the steps as a script. Experienced hosts can treat them as a foundation for variation and improvisation.**" — which is exactly your Simple-view / Advanced-view split, stated as a facilitation doctrine.

## The four convergent answers

**When to change the game:** DeKoven's *well-timed cheat* — "just when the game isn't as much fun as it should be, when people are getting too serious about winning or losing, when they are getting hurt." New Games treats it as routine: "The rules changed as necessary to accommodate different numbers and types of players." Spolin's subtler version: don't change the game, change the *coaching call*.
**Who owns the rules:** Boal — "the joker lays out the rules of the game, but the audience are free to change them." DeKoven — "the players decide if the game is good enough to play." New Games — "players became organizers, and organizers were players."
**How participation gets distributed:** LS makes it a mandatory spec field. Restorative circles use sequence order + a talking piece, where "**every person in the group is acknowledged, whether they speak or not**" — which defines participation as **being reached**, not as producing content. New Games ran **two balanced games in parallel** so quitting one always meant joining another.
**How the facilitator avoids becoming the center:** the leader is a player; the role rotates; the high-visibility referee's success is *leaving*; the Joker refuses to answer; LS's listener may only say "**What happened next?**"; Spolin lets **the game do the teaching**; Johnstone plays low status on purpose.

# PART 5 — Build recommendations, ranked

**Tier 1 — highest value per unit of work**
1. **The Mind → an `ascend` phase.** Purest shared-fate structure in print, architecturally identical to your shipped `one-voice`, scales to 30 where the cardboard caps at 4, and reskins into any ordering-based curriculum review.
2. **Just One → `engine/phases/clue-cancel.js` + existing collect/simultaneousReveal/foreach.** Already a text game. The duplicate-cancellation rule is the best social mechanic in this entire report, and it doubles as retrieval practice.
3. **`revealTail` on `rotateFrom`.** One small feature turns a shipped primitive into true Exquisite Corpse — plus the grammatical-template variant (needs nothing) and Renga/tanrenga (real ELA cover).

**Tier 2**
Mind Meld (simultaneity is *guaranteed* on a server — strictly better than in person) · 25/10 Crowd Sourcing (LS wrote it off as un-digitisable; your architecture makes it easy, min group size 15 matches a class exactly) · Warm Shower appreciation phase (90 appreciations, 16 minutes, zero students missed) · Troika Consulting (the turn-away is free and perfect in text) · TRIZ (funniest thing here, pure text, zero AI, safest way to let students critique a classroom) · Wavelength as a cooperative class-swarm on `estimate` · Fist-to-Five as a 6-button temperature check · Min Specs on inverted `checklist` machinery.

**Tier 3 — operators, not games (cheapest wins in the whole report)**
- `onEliminate: "convert"` on the eliminate phase (Blob/Prui/Monarch) — the player joins the other side with a *different action set* instead of watching.
- `oddHandling: "caller"` on pairwise phases (People to People) — the unpaired student gets the authoring seat.
- `autoDefect` on two-sided phases (Earthball) — whoever's ahead loses the next joiner.
- "Give each other points" as a scoring mode (DeKoven's #4).
- **Constructive-quitting roles** attached to Pass: coach / scorekeeper / timekeeper / recruiter / sportscaster / cheerer. You already have `passAllowed`; this makes the pass a *role* rather than an absence.
- **"Quitting Practice"** as an opening beat in the Connection Pack — a 30-second phase early in any activity where students are asked to tap Pass *even though they don't want to*, just to make the permission real.
- **Six Agreements / norms screen** (Conversation Café) as a reusable 30-second host component.
- **`requireAllBeforeSecond`** on collect — everyone contributes once before anyone contributes twice.
- **Shuffle every roster per device** (Poulin & Dishion) — this should be a validator rule, not a preference.
- **Teacher "these 3 students have zero" panel** during any appreciation phase, with a backfill route before close. This is the thing every practitioner in the literature was manually patching with popsicle sticks.

**Don't build:** Zip Zap Zop, Pass the Clap, Whoosh (embodied, directional, latency-bound — they degrade into generic timers). Full Hanabi (2-5 players, 30-40 min, steep curve — steal the clue budget instead). Codenames Duet (2-player; steal the asymmetric key). Ecocycle Planning (90 min, 2D canvas — degenerate it to a 4-bucket `sort`). Wise Crowds (whole-class spotlight on one adolescent). Conversation Café (spoken; steal the Six Agreements and the everyone-once rule). And **no "who is MOST X" activity, ever** — the harm vector is the public zero count.

**Two cautions for the docs**
- **PEDAGOGY.md:** the synchrony-prosociality literature is real (60-experiment meta-analysis, medium effect) but contested — there's a null result in a school field experiment, an active expectancy-effects critique, and **nobody has tested network-mediated tapping.** Claim the structure, not the neuroscience.
- **The movement's own post-mortem is aimed straight at you:** New Games died in part because *"it largely became based on a fixed, and eventually closed repertoire of games."* The teacher-authoring layer isn't a nice-to-have on top of a game library — per DeKoven, it's the thing the library exists to serve.
